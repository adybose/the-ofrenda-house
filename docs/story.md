# The Ofrenda House: story bible

Source of truth for the storyline. Specs reference this file; if a spec and this file disagree, raise it before coding.

## Pitch

On the night of 1 November, a stranger takes shelter from a storm in an abandoned mansion. The door locks behind them. Before dawn they must work out which member of the household poisoned the rest. The dead give them clues, one of them is lying, and a faceless shadow hunts them through a house that never ends, wherever the light gives out. Every spirit's voice comes from a local open model, and every room, light and sound is generated by code.

## Backstory (the hidden truth)

In 1931 a wealthy family lived in a hillside mansion outside a small town. Over one autumn, the household died one by one of a "wasting fever". The town blamed the house. In truth, one member of the household was poisoning them out of greed, using a tincture brewed from a white flower in the greenhouse.

On the last night, the dying spirits rose together and dragged the poisoner down the cellar stairs. The poisoner died hating them and became **La Sombra**, a faceless shadow bound to the house. Since then the house has trapped every trespasser and kept the dead from leaving. The only survivor was a baby, Elena, sent away to relatives before the last death. The player is Elena's grandchild, which is revealed only in the true ending.

## The household (rebuilt every run)

Each run creates a new household. Code draws 7 or 8 characters from a pool of roles and gives each a name, a personality, a room and a riddle theme. Then it gives 3 or 4 of them a **hidden identity or hidden agenda**. The killer is always one of the characters with a secret, so every suspect has a believable motive, and the player has to work out which secret led to murder. The names below come from one example run.

| Role | In every run? | Possible hidden secret (and motive) | Example run |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Patriarch | Always: the first victim and later your guide | None, never the killer | Don Aurelio, collector of clocks, study |
| Painter of the portraits | Always: makes the reveal in the true ending | Is the patriarch's illegitimate child, a hidden heir | Lucía, the daughter, studio |
| Matriarch | Always | Is in love with someone else in the house and planned to leave with the money | Doña Inés, keeper of the greenhouse |
| Bookkeeper | Usually | Stole from the estate and wanted his share of the will before the books were checked | Tomás, the nephew, library |
| Housekeeper or cook | Usually | Was the secret lover of the patriarch or matriarch, and was promised an inheritance that never came | Rosa, servants' stairs |
| Eldest child | Usually | Found out they were adopted and would be cut out of the will | Mateo, the pianist, music room |
| Family doctor | Sometimes | Has gambling debts, and prescribed the household's "tonic" | Dr. Salvador Reyes, guest room |
| Grandmother | Sometimes | Hides a family scandal, which makes her a red herring and never the killer | Abuela Carmen, kitchen |
| Gardener | Sometimes | Is the true heir from the patriarch's first marriage | Not drawn this run |

Each character with a secret who isn't the killer is a **red herring**: their secret is real, but another spirit holds a clue that clears them (an alibi, or proof they never touched the poison).

The case file records the killer, motive, means (the poison) and opportunity, and decides which spirits saw what. The killer's friendly form tells exactly one lie, which contradicts a true fact held by another spirit. Every other spirit holds 1 or 2 true facts.

## Prologue: the door

It's 11 pm on 1 November. Your car dies on a mountain road in a storm, and the only light is a mansion on the hill. As you reach for the knocker, the door swings open by itself. You step inside, it slams shut, and the lock turns. You pull at it and nothing happens. Lightning shows a hall of portraits for a moment, then darkness. You switch on your flashlight. Its battery is only about two-thirds charged, and it flickers.

## Act 1: the trespasser (tutorial, about 3 minutes)

You walk the portrait hall. Each flash of lightning shows faces that seem to have moved. A bat bursts from the chandelier (the first jump scare). Then the portrait at the end of the hall is empty, and the patriarch is standing behind you.

"Who are you? How dare you enter my house?" He chases you. You learn to run and turn, and the corridor shifts behind you until you're cornered in a dead end. He looms over you, then stops, studying your face. "Another one. The house took you, as it took the others. Find the way out before the last candle burns down, or you'll hang on these walls with them." He is gone, and the grandfather clock strikes midnight. You have until dawn.

You think to yourself: *There must be a candle somewhere here.* In the corner of the dead end stands an unlit candle in its stand, and beside it a box of matches. You take both.

## Act 2: the house of the dead (main loop, 15 to 25 minutes)

The house has no end. The key rooms (one per family member except the patriarch) are hidden among dozens of generated filler rooms (parlours, bedrooms, nurseries, bathrooms) and corridors that rearrange whenever lightning strikes. Most rooms hold no spirit. A spirit's room has its portrait on the wall and, in front of it, a small table with a candle in its stand, unlit. A few ordinary rooms hold a spare candle too. Some spirits' rooms are locked.

**Light.** Light is all that keeps you safe. Your flashlight began the night two-thirds charged and only runs down: it dims and flickers more as it fades, and once it is nearly flat it cuts out without warning, then comes back weak, dim and always flickering, never recovering past a tenth of its charge. The matchbox holds a handful of matches, each burning a few seconds: enough to see by, to reach a candle, or to light one. A candle burns for several minutes and can be carried (you can carry several, but hold only one, lit or not), set in any empty stand, blown out to save it, or used to light another. Once the matches are gone, only a burning candle can light another. No candle lasts the night.

**The spirits.** In each key room a spirit waits. It drifts in through the wall, floating above the floor, faintly visible even in the dark by the halo around it. It asks who you are and where you came from, and the answers you pick change how much it trusts you. Then it asks a riddle about its own life. Solve it and it gives you a **clue card** (motive, means, opportunity or alibi), then leaves through the wall, and the candle beneath its portrait lights by itself. Some spirits hold the key to another spirit's locked room: it appears beside the candle after the spirit has gone, for you to take. Fail and the good spirits fall silent. They won't hurt you, but that clue is lost, and the Shadow grows bolder.

**Naming the killer.** Once you hold enough clues, each spirit whose riddle you solve asks one more question: who do you think did this? Whatever you answer, it laughs loudly, leaves, and a key appears beside its candle. If you named the killer, it is the key to the way out. If not, it is the key to another spirit's locked room, while any are left. When none are left and you still haven't named the killer, all you can do is survive until dawn.

**La Sombra** hunts you. It whispers through the walls, drops a chandelier or a mirror where you're about to walk, slams doors to trap you, and kills your flashlight. If it comes across you while you have light (the flashlight, a match or a candle), it threatens you in its terrible voice and leaves you alone, for now. If it finds you in the dark, it chases you: outrun it, or strike a light, or reach a lit room, before it closes in. Never walk up to it by candlelight: a cold gust blows every flame out and it seizes you. If it seizes you, you have a heartbeat to strike a match or switch on your flashlight and throw it off; if you can't, you are lost. It never enters the room of a spirit you have set at peace while that room's candle burns, but if the candle burns out or you carry it away, the room is dark and nothing keeps it out. Rats scatter from under floorboards, cats scream from the dark and bats burst from fireplaces. These are scares, not threats.

**Your thoughts.** The stranger thinks aloud: short thoughts on screen about what they have understood and what to do next. They are the player's hints.

One of the friendly spirits is the killer in disguise. Their clue contradicts a fact from another spirit, and noticing which two cards don't agree is how the player catches them.

## Act 3: the way out (finale)

There are two ways out.

- **The key to the way out.** Name the killer to a spirit and the key it leaves opens the dining room, which appears at the end of the next corridor. On the table is an ofrenda with a candle and a portrait for each of the dead, and the patriarch waiting beside it. Beyond it, the garden door stands open to the night.
- **Dawn.** If you survive until the clock strikes six, La Sombra fades with the dark, and the patriarch appears before you once more. "Who did this?" You have one last chance. Name the killer and he laughs: "Well done, you found the killer. You are free now." The walls stop moving, marigold petals (cempasúchil) mark the way back, and you walk to the front door you came in by, and open it. Name the wrong one and he laughs: "Too bad, now you join the others who came before you."

- **True ending (killer named, and you told the spirits the truth about yourself):** The spirits turn on the Shadow and it burns away. The painter unveils their last painting, of baby Elena, and you recognise your grandmother's face. The patriarch lets you go. "Go home, child. Light a candle for us." Dawn breaks on 2 November.
- **Escape ending (killer named only):** The Shadow is bound and you walk free, but the spirits stay behind and the house will wait for the next trespasser.
- **Possessed ending (caught by the Shadow):** "You lost. You were possessed by the spirit of La Sombra." Your vision goes dark, and a new portrait hangs in the hall with your face.
- **Trapped ending (the wrong name at dawn):** The rooms stop moving, with you inside one of them forever, and a new portrait hangs in the hall with your face.

## Controls

| Action | Keys |
| --- | --- |
| Move and turn | Arrow keys (up and down to walk, left and right to turn) |
| Look up and down | Mouse, or Page Up and Page Down |
| Run | Shift |
| Interact (door, pick up, set down or light a candle) | E |
| Flashlight on or off | F |
| Strike a match (lights the candle you hold, if it's unlit) | M |
| Blow out the candle you hold | B |
| Clue journal | J |
| Pick an answer | Click the option, or press 1 to 4 |

The player never types. Every answer is picked from 3 or 4 suggested options.

## The encounter loop

1. You enter a room and the spirit appears. The LLM writes its opening line from its persona and how much it trusts you so far.
2. **Who are you?** The spirit asks about you, and you pick one of 3 or 4 replies, such as "A traveller lost in the storm", "Your family, I think" or "None of your business". Each reply shifts trust, and honest answers lead to the true ending.
3. **The riddle.** The spirit asks a riddle about its life, with 4 answers to choose from. The LLM writes the riddle and the 3 wrong answers, but the right answer comes from the case file, so the riddle can always be solved. A spirit that trusts you gives you a second try.
4. **Reward or silence.** Right answer: a clue card, the spirit leaves through the wall, its candle lights, and maybe a key appears beside the candle. Wrong answer: the good spirit fades without helping, and the Shadow's threat level rises.
5. **Who did it?** Once you hold enough clues, a spirit you have solved asks who you think the killer is, from the members of the household with secrets. It laughs loudly whatever you answer and leaves a key: to the way out if you were right, otherwise to another locked room, if any are left.

## What the LLM does and doesn't do

The model voices the spirits; it never runs the game. Code owns every fact and every rule. For each line the server sends the model the spirit's persona, the facts it may reveal, the player's last choice and a strict JSON output format (line, riddle, answer options). Code validates the output before anything appears on screen.

- **Spirits:** a persona card per character, with mood, speech style and 1931 period details. One shared system prompt keeps them in character.
- **The liar:** the killer's friendly form gets the same facts plus one instruction: misstate this one fact, convincingly.
- **La Sombra:** short taunts and threats during chases and confrontations, and whispers through the walls, under 12 words.
- **Not the stranger's thoughts:** the on-screen thoughts are hand-written hints, so they are always right.
- **Voices:** every spirit line is spoken aloud through local text-to-speech (Piper), pitched and echoed so it sounds ghostly.
- **Fallbacks:** each role ships with hand-written lines and riddles, so the game runs even if Ollama or Piper is missing.
- **Latency:** the next room's dialogue is generated in the background while the player walks, and text streams in as typewriter output.

## Atmosphere must-haves

- True 3D first-person view.
- Darkness everywhere outside the flashlight's focused beam, which casts real-time shadows.
- Lightning flashing through tall windows, throwing window-frame shadows across the floor, followed by thunder.
- Sounds: rain, wind, thunder, creaking doors, keys locking and unlocking, footsteps, heartbeat, scare stingers, echoing spirit whispers, spoken spirit voices.
- Jump scares from bats, rats and cats, plus the Shadow's hazards.
- Light as the only safety: a fading flashlight, matches and candles, and the warm pools of a spirit's candle in the dark.
- Spirits glowing faintly with a halo, floating, passing through walls, and laughing loudly when they ask their last question.
- Visual style: dark, foggy, grainy, lo-fi (PS1-era) so procedural art reads as horror.
